**Item 1:**
*The student is able to critique conceptual accuracy and logical coherence in a reaction paper on **Automated Item Generation (AIG)**.*

**Response Criteria:**
- **Level 0 (None):** The reaction paper fails to address conceptual accuracy or logical coherence. No evidence of engagement with the topic’s psychometric or methodological implications (e.g., ignores item validity, bias, or theoretical underpinnings of AIG).
- **Level 1 (Limited):** The critique touches on *some* aspects of conceptual accuracy or coherence (e.g., mentions "automated items" or "LLM limitations") but lacks depth, specificity, or critical analysis. Errors include:
  - Superficial observations (e.g., "It’s fast" without linking to psychometric standards).
  - Logical inconsistencies (e.g., conflating AIG with human-generated items without distinction).
  - No engagement with empirical or theoretical critiques (e.g., ignores sampling bias, content validity, or fairness concerns).
- **Level 2 (Moderate):** The critique demonstrates *mostly* accurate and coherent analysis of AIG’s conceptual and logical coherence, with minor flaws. Examples of competence:
  - Identifies key psychometric risks (e.g., "AIG may overrepresent certain item types, skewing difficulty estimates").
  - Discusses trade-offs (e.g., "Speed vs. reliability trade-offs in automated item pools").
  - Minor errors include:
    - Overgeneralizing findings (e.g., applying AIG critiques to unrelated models without nuance).
    - Incomplete references to validation methods (e.g., omits pilot testing or expert review).
- **Level 3 (Complete):** The critique is *fully* accurate, coherent, and comprehensive, demonstrating mastery of AIG’s conceptual and logical challenges. Evidence includes:
  - Rigorous engagement with psychometric standards (e.g., "AIG items must meet IRT assumptions; failure to do so risks biased parameter estimates").
  - Critical evaluation of logical coherence (e.g., "The LLM’s training data may introduce content gaps, violating construct representation").
  - Integration of empirical evidence (e.g., cites studies on AIG’s reliability or fairness) and methodological solutions (e.g., "Hybrid review processes can mitigate bias").
  - No observable errors; analysis is nuanced, evidence-based, and aligned with course readings.

**Item 2:**
*The student is able to critique conceptual accuracy and logical coherence in a reaction paper on **Automated Item Generation (AIG)**.*

**Response Criteria:**
- **Level 0 (None):** The reaction paper provides no critique of conceptual accuracy or logical coherence. The discussion is either absent, irrelevant, or reduces AIG to a technical description (e.g., "Here’s how to prompt an LLM") without addressing psychometric validity.
- **Level 1 (Limited):** The critique attempts to address accuracy or coherence but fails in critical areas. Examples of incomplete execution:
  - Focuses on *procedural* details (e.g., "The LLM needs fine-tuning") without linking to conceptual validity.
  - Logical flaws include:
    - Assuming AIG automatically solves psychometric problems (e.g., "LLMs generate unbiased items").
    - Ignoring domain-specific knowledge (e.g., "AIG works for all constructs" without evidence).
  - No engagement with theoretical frameworks (e.g., IRT, CTT) or empirical limitations.
- **Level 2 (Moderate):** The critique accurately addresses *most* aspects of conceptual accuracy and logical coherence, with isolated weaknesses. Strengths include:
  - Discusses specific psychometric risks (e.g., "AIG may produce items with low discriminant validity").
  - Addresses logical gaps (e.g., "The model’s training data may not align with the target population").
  - Minor errors include:
    - Underestimating the complexity of validation (e.g., "Pilot testing is optional").
    - Superficial references to fairness (e.g., "AIG could be biased" without mechanisms or data).
- **Level 3 (Complete):** The critique is a *masterful* evaluation of AIG’s conceptual and logical coherence, demonstrating advanced psychometric and methodological insight. Key indicators:
  - **Conceptual Accuracy:** Critiques align with established psychometric theory (e.g., "AIG violates unidimensionality if items tap multiple constructs").
  - **Logical Coherence:** Identifies and resolves paradoxes (e.g., "AIG’s speed may compromise construct relevance unless guided by item response theory").
  - **Evidence Integration:** Uses course readings to support claims (e.g., cites [Author, Year] on AIG’s reliability or [Study] on content validity pitfalls).
  - **Critical Nuance:** Distinguishes between AIG’s strengths (e.g., scalability) and weaknesses (e.g., "Without human oversight, AIG may overfit to training data").
  - **Actionable Insights:** Proposes solutions (e.g., "Hybrid models combining AIG and expert review can balance efficiency and validity").

**Key Differentiators Between Items:**
- **Item 1** emphasizes *depth of psychometric engagement* (e.g., IRT assumptions, empirical evidence).

Both adhere strictly to the stem and topic while targeting distinct observable variables (e.g., **Item 1**: "rigorous engagement with standards"; **Item 2**: "critical nuance and paradox resolution").
